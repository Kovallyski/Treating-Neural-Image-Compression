# Adversarial attack on neural image compression

## Demo

With Python 3.10 or newer, install the dependencies and run the Cheng2020 attention demo on the included image:

```bash
pip install -r requirements.txt
python demo_cheng_attack.py --image demo.png --quality 6
```

The script selects CUDA when available and otherwise uses the CPU. On the first run, CompressAI may download the pretrained codec weights. Results are written to `demo_outputs/`: the original and adversarial inputs, clean and attacked reconstructions, an amplified perturbation image, and `losses.csv`. Add `--frequency_mask` to run the adaptive frequency-filtering variant; use `--out_dir` to keep its outputs separate.

### A.1 Attack invisibility-efficiency trade-off

Figure 4 illustrates the trade-off between invisibility and compression efficiency degradation (CED-SCORE). The results show that our proposed method consistently achieves higher CED-SCORE values at comparable levels of invisibility than baseline attacks, indicating a more effective balance between stealth and destructive impact. In particular, the frequency-domain masking variant attains the best compromise: it maintains imperceptible perturbations while still inducing significant efficiency losses in the codecs. This demonstrates that the modular design not only enhances attack strength but also improves its practicality by minimizing visual detectability.

In this section, we show the results of an experiment on the KODAK PhotoCD dataset with a smaller perturbation budget ε = 5/255 (and ε = 0.0003 for FTDA and SRDA (ℓ₂)). The results in Table 2 show that with a lower ε, attacks become much less effective, but our methods still show the best results.

**Table 2.** Comparison of attack performance across all tested NIC codecs on the KODAK PhotoCD dataset with perturbation budget ε = 5/255. QD-SCORE (quality degradation) and CED-SCORE (compression efficiency degradation) are reported for PSNR and VMAF. The proposed method consistently achieves higher degradation with lower visibility than baseline attacks.

### A.4 Variations of the P parameter

| P | 25 | 50 | 75 | 100 | 125 | 150 |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| PSNR | 0.157 | 0.189 | 0.188 | 0.188 | 0.181 | 0.177 |
| VMAF | 0.087 | 0.090 | 0.090 | 0.091 | 0.088 | 0.089 |

**Table 3.** QD-SCORE of our attack for different P values on the JPEG AI codec family using the KODAK dataset.

The parameter P controls the trade-off between artifact localization granularity and computational efficiency. Empirical findings during development showed that P ∈ [50, 100] gives approximately the same results. It makes no sense to increase the parameter, since the current size is sufficient for local artifacts to occur, and increasing it will only lead to the loss of some of them. We selected P = 75 as the midpoint providing optimal balance.

### A.5 Coverage Interpretation of the Local Objective

We now show that the local objective in Eq. (10) has a direct artifact-coverage interpretation. Let the image be divided into B non-overlapping blocks and assume 0 ≤ m_b ≤ U. Define

![Definitions of m_b as the maximum degradation in block b, f_local as the sum of block maxima, and C(τ) as the fraction of blocks with m_b ≥ τ](supplementary/images/local-objective-definitions.png)

Here, m_b is the maximum local degradation score in block b, and C(τ) measures how many spatial blocks contain an artifact whose strength is at least τ. Since the indicator is equal to one exactly on the interval [0, m_b], for any m_b ∈ [0, U] we have

![Derivation showing that m_b is the integral of its threshold indicator, and f_local divided by B equals the integral of C(τ) from 0 to U](supplementary/images/local-objective-coverage.png)

Thus, the normalized local objective is exactly the area under the artifact-coverage curve. Maximizing f_local therefore does not only increase the strongest local degradation values, but also encourages artifacts to appear across many spatial blocks and across a range of degradation thresholds.
